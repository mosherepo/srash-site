Point of view

The database is the slowest thing in your stack to change and the most expensive to get wrong. We treat every upgrade and migration as a rehearsed, reversible change, with the rollback path built before the cutover.

7 services, each with a fixed-fee entry point.

Relational, NoSQL and in-memory. On premises, AWS, Azure, Google Cloud and OCI. Each is scoped after a free 30-minute review, and the assessment fee is credited to the project.

01 / 07

Database Upgrades & End-of-Support Programs

Every engine has a clock. RDS and Cloud SQL now bill for extended support on old PostgreSQL and MySQL versions, SQL Server 2017 support ends in October 2027 and MongoDB 7.0 in August 2027. We plan, rehearse and run the upgrades on a fixed fee.

Sound familiar?

  • Your AWS bill now carries RDS Extended Support charges for MySQL 8.0 or PostgreSQL 13, and PostgreSQL 14 joins them on February 28, 2027.
  • You still run SQL Server 2016, which left extended support in July 2026, or SQL Server 2017, which follows on October 13, 2027.
  • MongoDB 6.0 is already past end of life, 7.0 ends on August 31, 2027, and nobody has tested the drivers against 8.0.

You receive

  • Inventory of versions, support dates and extended-support cost
  • Upgrade path and runbook per database
  • Compatibility test results

02 / 07

Database Migration & Modernization

Licence renewals, data centre exits and consolidation programs all end in the same place: moving data without breaking the business. We assess every database honestly, move only what pays back and validate every row before cut-over.

Sound familiar?

  • An Oracle or SQL Server renewal is coming and finance wants to know what a move to PostgreSQL would really cost.
  • You are leaving a data centre or VMware and dozens of databases need a home on AWS, Azure or Google Cloud.
  • Years of growth left you with too many small instances, engines and versions to secure and patch.

You receive

  • Assessment with a target and effort per database
  • Licence and run-cost comparison
  • Wave plan with dependencies

03 / 07

NoSQL Engineering: MongoDB, Cassandra, Aerospike, DynamoDB & Cosmos DB

NoSQL clusters run quietly until a version leaves support, a partition gets hot or the bill doubles. We upgrade, re-model, migrate and tune MongoDB, Cassandra, Aerospike, DynamoDB and Cosmos DB with the same discipline we bring to relational systems.

Sound familiar?

  • MongoDB 6.0 is already out of support and 7.0 ends on August 31, 2027; you want to land on 8.0 without surprises.
  • Your Cassandra 3.11 ring has been unsupported since September 2024 and the upgrade must go one major version at a time.
  • Aerospike 7.x releases reached end of life during 2025 and 2026, and the team that built the cluster has moved on.

You receive

  • Health check report with prioritized findings
  • Version path and rolling-upgrade runbook
  • Data model recommendations

04 / 07

Redis, Valkey & Caching Engineering

Redis changed its licence in 2024, Valkey forked under the Linux Foundation, and Microsoft is retiring Azure Cache for Redis. We help you choose between Redis and Valkey, move caches without dropped sessions and design caching that holds up under load.

Sound familiar?

  • Legal flagged the Redis licence change and wants to know whether Valkey removes the question.
  • You run Azure Cache for Redis Enterprise, which retires on March 31, 2027, or Basic, Standard or Premium, which retire on September 30, 2028.
  • ElastiCache for Valkey costs less than Redis OSS and you want the saving without a risky cut-over.

You receive

  • Inventory and licence position
  • Target recommendation with cost comparison
  • Migration runbook with rollback

05 / 07

Database Performance & Reliability Engineering

Slow queries, failovers that have never been tested and backups nobody has restored are the quiet risks in most estates. We find the real bottlenecks, prove recovery works and leave your team with numbers instead of hunches.

Sound familiar?

  • Peak traffic makes the database the bottleneck and the answer so far has been a bigger instance.
  • Your recovery time and recovery point objectives are written down but have never been tested.
  • A failover happened once and took far longer than anyone expected.

You receive

  • Prioritized findings with expected impact
  • Tuned queries, indexes and configuration
  • HA/DR design and failover test results

How we deliver Data & Databases work

The same five phases on every engagement in this practice, so you always know what happens next and what you hold at the end of each phase.

  1. 01

    Discover

    Inventory engines, versions, sizes, dependencies and support dates from read-only access or exports.

  2. 02

    Assess

    Compatibility, performance baselines, backup and restore proof, and a risk register.

  3. 03

    Plan

    Waves, windows, rollback and a fixed quote, in a written plan you keep.

  4. 04

    Rehearse

    Every change runs end to end in a production-like environment, with measured timings.

  5. 05

    Cut over and hand over

    Executed in your window, verified against agreed checks, documented in your repository.

Outcomes we measure

How we frame the business case. We agree the baseline and the measure in the statement of work and report against them. We never quote invented benchmarks or other clients' results. Building the business case

  1. Support exposure removed

    Instances moved off end-of-support versions, and the extended-support fees avoided, calculated from the vendor's published rates and your own inventory.

  2. Downtime per change

    Planned downtime measured in rehearsal, then reported against the actual window after each cut-over.

  3. Recovery proven

    Restore time and data-loss window (RTO and RPO) tested, not assumed, and written into the runbook.

  4. Performance against baseline

    p95 latency, throughput and resource use for your critical queries, before and after.

  5. Licence and run cost

    Licence and infrastructure cost after a migration, taken from your actual contracts and bills.

Questions about Data & Databases

Straight answers on scope, fees and how we work. Anything else, ask us.

Ask a question

Which database engines do you work with?

PostgreSQL, MySQL, SQL Server and Oracle, their managed versions (Aurora, RDS, Azure SQL, Cloud SQL, AlloyDB, Spanner), and MongoDB, Cassandra, Aerospike, Redis, Valkey, DynamoDB and Cosmos DB.

Are you a DBA agency?

No. We are an engineering firm. We upgrade, migrate, design and automate data platforms, and run them SRE-style when you want that, with a named senior engineer rather than a ticket queue.

Do you need production access?

Not for assessments. Read-only access or exports are usually enough, and we sign your NDA first.

Do you offer 24x7 support?

No. Managed services are business hours by default, with extended hours agreed in writing for specific windows.

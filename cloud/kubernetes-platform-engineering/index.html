Kubernetes & Platform Engineering

Kubernetes ships a new minor version roughly every four months and managed services retire old ones on a fixed calendar. We keep clusters supported, build platforms developers actually like using and remove the toil around them.

Book a free 30-minute review How it is scoped

Dates that drive this work

  1. Nov 26, 2026  days Amazon EKS Kubernetes 1.31 leaves EKS extended support; remaining clusters are auto-upgraded.ics
  2. Nov 2026  days Azure AKS Kubernetes 1.34 reaches end of life on AKS (community support).ics
  3. Dec 2, 2026  days Amazon EKS Kubernetes 1.34 ends standard support on EKS; extended-support billing starts.ics
  4. Jan 25, 2027  days Google GKE Kubernetes 1.34 ends standard support on GKE.ics

Sources: Microsoft Learn: Supported Kubernetes versions in AKS, AWS: Kubernetes version lifecycle on Amazon EKS, Google Cloud: GKE release schedule

Sound familiar?

  • EKS clusters are paying for extended support, or will be auto-upgraded when it ends.
  • AKS and GKE will upgrade clusters on their own schedule if you do not, and deprecated APIs will break on the way.
  • Every team built its own cluster and pipeline, and nobody can upgrade them all.
  • Developers wait days for environments that should take minutes.

Questions about this service

Straight answers on scope, fees and how we work. Anything else, ask us.

Ask a question

What happens when EKS extended support ends?

AWS says clusters still on the version are auto-upgraded to the oldest version in extended support. Extended support itself is billed per cluster-hour on top of the standard rate.

Can we skip Kubernetes versions?

Control planes upgrade one minor version at a time on EKS and for supported AKS clusters. The review plans the sequence and the add-on changes each step needs.

Do you support OpenShift and OKE?

Yes, alongside EKS, AKS and GKE.

Is the review fee credited?

Yes, in full, toward the upgrade if you go ahead.

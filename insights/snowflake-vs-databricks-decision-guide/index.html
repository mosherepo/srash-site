Snowflake or Databricks? A practical decision guide for data leaders

Both platforms now do most of what the other does. The useful question is which one fits your workloads, your team and your cost model, and whether open formats let you avoid choosing at all.

By the SRASH team · November 15, 2026 · 7 min read

Key facts

  • Both platforms now support Apache Iceberg: Snowflake through Iceberg tables, Databricks through Delta Lake with UniForm and managed Iceberg.
  • Governance lives in Unity Catalog on Databricks and Snowflake Horizon on Snowflake.
  • Both bill by consumption, so engineering choices drive the bill more than list prices do.
  • Microsoft-centred estates should compare both with Microsoft Fabric, especially where Power BI capacity is changing.

Start from the workloads, not the brochure

Five years ago the choice was simple: Snowflake for SQL analytics, Databricks for Spark engineering and machine learning. Today both run SQL warehouses, Python, streaming, machine learning and AI features. Feature lists no longer decide it. Your workloads, your team and how you want to pay do.

QuestionOften points to SnowflakeOften points to Databricks
Main usersSQL analysts and analytics engineersData engineers and data scientists writing Python and Spark
Main workloadsBI, reporting, ELT with dbt, data sharingLarge-scale engineering, streaming, machine learning
Operations appetitePrefer a managed service with few settingsComfortable tuning clusters, jobs and storage
StorageNative tables, or Iceberg tables in your storageDelta Lake in your cloud storage, readable as Iceberg
GovernanceSnowflake HorizonUnity Catalog

These are tendencies, not rules. Plenty of SQL-first teams run well on Databricks SQL, and plenty of engineering teams run Snowpark on Snowflake.

Open table formats change the question

Snowflake can create and query Apache Iceberg tables in your own storage, and Databricks can expose Delta tables to Iceberg clients through UniForm. That makes it realistic to keep one copy of the data and let more than one engine read it. It also adds catalog, permission and performance questions, so running both platforms should be a decision with a reason, not a default.

Cost: compare workloads, not list prices

Both platforms bill by consumption: Snowflake in credits for warehouses and serverless features, Databricks in DBUs plus your cloud compute. The same workload can cost very different amounts depending on warehouse or cluster size, auto-suspend, incremental processing and query design. The only fair comparison is a proof of concept on a representative slice of your own workloads, measured the same way on both.

Where Microsoft Fabric fits

Microsoft Fabric combines data engineering, warehousing, real-time analytics and Power BI on one capacity model. For organisations already standardised on Microsoft, and especially those moving Power BI Premium P SKUs to Fabric F SKUs or leaving Azure Synapse, Fabric belongs on the shortlist.

Questions to answer before you choose

  1. Which five workloads matter most in the next 12 months, and who builds them?
  2. What skills does your team have, and what can it hire for in your market?
  3. Where must the data live, and which tools outside the platform need to read it?
  4. How will you govern access, lineage and sensitive data from the first day?
  5. How will you control cost: budgets, monitors, policies and who watches them?

How we help

We hold no partner status with Snowflake or Databricks and resell no credits. Our platform architecture sprint compares the options on your own workloads and writes the decision down. If you already run one of them, the cost health check is usually the fastest way to see what the platform should cost.
